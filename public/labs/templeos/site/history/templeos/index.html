History · The founder

Terry A. Davis and the creation of TempleOS.

One programmer wrote an entire operating system, a language, a compiler and its games over more than a decade, and put it in the public domain.

Photographs

Four photographs from Wikimedia Commons, all marked public domain there. Dates are the ones given on each file page.

The founder

1969
Terry Allen Davis is born on 15 December in West Allis, Wisconsin.
1990s
He trains as an electrical engineer and works as a programmer, including a short stint at Ticketmaster writing operating-system code. Sources disagree on which university gave his master's degree.
Later years
He lives with serious mental illness and, in his own account, understood his work as a commission from God to build a temple in software.
2018
He dies on 11 August at the age of 48.

The creation

Early work
A line of private operating systems precedes the public one. The Wikipedia article puts the start of the project at 2003; an archived timeline traces a first system, TPMOS, back to 1994.
c. 2005
The first public release, as the J Operating System.
2008
Renamed LoseThos.
2013
Renamed SparrowOS, and then, the same year, TempleOS.
2017
The last release, after which Davis kept the system in his own hands until his death.

What he built

TempleOS is a 64-bit, ring-0, single-address-space system that always runs at 640x480 with 16 colours. Everything is written in HolyC, a language of his own design that is compiled as you type and used for both programs and the command line. The system came with a compiler, editor, graphics library, games, an "oracle" and its own document format, DolDoc, in about a hundred thousand lines of code. He placed all of it in the public domain.

Why it matters here

The Kosmos Temple Desktop keeps every one of those constraints. It is HolyC on the same kernel, drawing into the same framebuffer. The history of this desktop picks up where Davis stopped.

Sources: Terry A. Davis and TempleOS on Wikipedia, and an archived timeline of TempleOS. Sources disagree on several dates; this page flags them, and they are worth checking against primary material before this site is published.
