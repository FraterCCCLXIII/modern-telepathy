Kamiji on the HolyC TempleOS desktop and on Aiwnios: study

Edited 2026-10-27docs/kamiji-on-templeos.md

Date 2026-10-27, branch task/templeos-shell. Research only. Nothing was built, installed, downloaded or pulled; no LFS command was used (sizes are file sizes on disk). The only code run was a 15-line Python script that reads GLB headers.

Labels: [V] read in a file or measured today. [R] measured in our earlier notes. [E] my estimate from measured numbers. [I] inference or memory. Effort: S under a day, M a week, L a month.

1. What Kamiji is#

1.1 Answer#

Kamiji is a family. I infer the Projects entry "Kamiji: Rigged 3D hand avatars and the characters around them" means the characters: a rigged cartoon hand with a morph-target face, the Kamikin "mochi" friends and the Rubbernose cast built with the same toolchain. Confidence about 85 percent. "Kamiji UI" is separate: the Longformer lf-* component kit that Kamiji's prototype folder carries (section 4). The game repos are ancestors, not the product.

Why: apps/os-styles/projects.json has three character rows (Kamiji, Kamikin, Hands) and one game row (Kamae, prototype); no row is called "Kamiji UI" [V]. The kamiji README opens "A rigged, expressive 3D hand avatar for React, built on three.js" [V].

1.2 Repo map (under /Users/paulbloch/Documents/github/)#

Grepping the engine for kamiji finds only docs, licences, apps/os-styles and our own docs. There is no Rust Kamiji runtime: poses, expressions and motion are TypeScript in the kamiji repo (kamiji-pose-tween.ts, kamiji-expressions.ts, kamiji-motion.ts) [V]. The engine's kosmos-model/src/hands.rs targets Mixamo-style bones (mixamorig:LeftHandIndex1..4); Kamiji's skeleton is Root > Palm > {Thumb,Index,Middle,Ring,Pinky}_1..3 plus Face [V]. The engine does consume mochi data (mochi_import.rs, skinned body, face, baked motions) and any GLB (gltf_import.rs, kosmos-app --glb, with --clip, --at, --screenshot [V main.rs:22]). docs/plans/kamikin-port.md covers the Kamikin game and toon look in kosmos-app.

1.3 The assets#

From a script that reads the GLB JSON chunk (.../scratchpad/kamiji/glbinfo.py) [V]:

Asset Size Verts Tris Joints Morphs Clips
kamiji.glb hand + face 2.36 MB 27,437 (hand 26,452) 43,514 (hand 41,744) 18 face 22, hand 2 (shapeSphere, shapeCube) Idle, Wave, Think, Celebrate (2 to 3 s)
mochi/kamikin-pip.glb 634 KB 5,526 10,818 15 none idle, walk, run, hop, happy, wave, sad, sit, dance
mochi/kamikin-chochin.glb 1.39 MB 16,069 31,946 8 none same nine

The kamiji repo holds 265 GLBs, 50 MB (rubbernose 25 MB for 16 characters, mochi 11 MB, props 11 MB). Mochi faces are six embedded PNGs mapped by extras.kamiji.faceStates. The 14 hand poses (open fist point peace thumbsUp rock three one two four five ok thumbsDown callMe) are curl tables in TypeScript, not in the GLB; so are the eight expressions, 15 emotes and three shapes [V]. Rendering is cel shading with an inverted-hull ink outline and flat bands [V kamiji-toon.ts]. That is flat colour in a few tones, a good match for a 16-colour palette and a small software rasteriser.

1.4 Licences [V kamiji/LICENSE.md, assets/PROVENANCE.md]#

Code: MIT. Characters and assets: CC BY-NC 4.0 with credit "Kamiji by Paul Bloch"; for-profit use needs a paid Premium Licence. "Kamiji" and "Kamikin" are trademarks. The licence itself lists open items: the hand's base mesh came from Meshy AI and its terms "must permit the licences above before any public release"; Kamikin provenance rows say TODO; twelve mochi exports are git-ignored until cleared. Treat all of it as local use only until the owner clears provenance.

1.5 How it connects to Kosmos and agents#

The GLB is copied into the engine; kamiji/tools/mochi-export/export.mjs turns engine mochi folders into GLBs. The control surface is small and suits us. Protocol v1 is one JSON object, {"v":1,"agent":"hermes-1","status":"running","mood":"thinking","color":"#4aa8ff","emote":"sparkle","ttl":5000}, with statuses idle running paused waiting finished error stuck, eight moods, 15 emotes, a JSON Schema and a mapping table. integrations/mcp/server.js exposes it over stdio to a loopback bridge; its README says it works end to end in headless Chrome with SwiftShader WebGL [V]. A desktop Characters app can take the same message over our hub in under 200 bytes. The engine's own MCP surface (scene_load, sim_step, screenshot, hashes) addresses scenes, not Kamiji state, and agent-native.md still lists headless screenshot as a gap [V].

2. The characters in the OS#

Goal: a window showing a skinned character or hand with clips, poses, expressions, pointer interaction and a screenshot, on x86 TempleOS under QEMU and on Aiwnios, from one HolyC source.

2.1 Viewport and Scene today [V desk3/03-apps.HC]#

  • Viewport (app 1, lines 38 to 69): 8 cube vertices, 12 edges, F64 Sin/Cos, perspective divide, Line(). Two cubes and a label. No mesh loader, depth, fill or input.
  • Scene (app 3, lines 80 to 96): static text and a tick counter. No state link.
  • Dispatch is AppBody; NA is 16 and ids 14 and 15 are Weather and Music, so a new app needs NA raised [V 01-core.HC:26]. Other agents have uncommitted edits in 01-core.HC, 02-ui.HC, 03-apps.HC, 04-main.HC; follow the Clock-suite pattern: new files plus one-line hooks.
  • Available: Line, RRFill, Disk, Copy32, clip rectangles, a 32-bit framebuffer, boot-image assets (KSICONS.BIN, KSFONT2.BIN), the style editors [V].

2.2 Cost model#

Measured numbers from holyc-improvement-analysis.md [R]: simple integer op 1 to 2 ns; call 9 to 12 ns; pixel by pointer walk 3 ns; F64 on x87 67 ns per multiply-add; 16.16 fixed point 2 ns. The brief's "30 ns per op" matches neither integers (1 to 3) nor F64 (67); I use measured values and show a 30 ns pessimistic case. Aiwnios: window drag costs 1.5 ms there against 5.7 ms on QEMU, about 4x [R aiwnios/README.md]. I found no repo evidence for 40 to 60x; I assume 4x to 10x for compute [E]. Aiwnios has no inline asm, U64 multiply truncates to 32 bits, F64 is IEEE not x87; only fixed-point integer maths gives identical pixels on both builds [R].

Unit costs, x86 TCG, fixed point [E]: vertex (two-bone skin, project, 2 divides) 150 ns; triangle (cull, edge setup, 3 calls) 300 ns; pixel (flat span, z compare) 5 ns, 8 ns with three-band shade.

Scene Compute x1 With 3x margin At 30 ns/op (about 15x)
Hand decimated to 1,500 tris, 800 verts, 140x140 px 0.7 ms 2 ms 30 ms (30 fps)
Pip decimated to 3,000 tris, 192x192 1.6 ms 5 ms 70 ms (14 fps)
Pip as exported, 10,818 tris, 256x256 4.5 ms 14 ms (40 fps) 200 ms
Hand as exported, 41,744 tris 17 ms 50 ms (20 fps) seconds

Reading: on x86 the compute of a decimated character is small beside the desktop's own present cost (5.6 ms for a window drag [R]). The un-decimated hand is out of reach on x86, so every guest-drawn route needs decimated rigs. On Aiwnios the same code should be 4x to 10x cheaper, so a 10k-triangle mochi at 60 fps is plausible [E]. Palette: toon shading needs 3 or 4 tones per material, so 8 to 16 entries per character; tint becomes a palette swap at no per-pixel cost.

2.3 Routes#

R1. Host engine renders, guest shows tiles. kosmos-app --glb kamiji.glb --clip Wave --at T --screenshot out.png (KOSMOS_SHOT_SIZE=WxH) renders the real toon look [V]. The tile channel exists: apps-host/stream.py sends EA_TILE (x,y,w,h; enc 0 = 16-colour palette with RLE, two pixels a byte; enc 1 = RGB565 RLE) and takes EA_INPUT back, lockstep with an ACK per chunk [V]. - Bandwidth at 16 KB/s: a raw 256x256 4-bpp frame is 32 KB, 2 s. Toon frames are flat; I guess 4 to 8 KB after RLE [E], so 2 to 4 fps full frame, about 8 fps for a 128x128 dirty rect (2 KB). Our notes quote 47 KB/s, unreproduced, which triples that. Input round trip 100 to 250 ms. - Aiwnios: each host call is a KsExec process spawn (hostlink-aiw.HC) [V]. Better: the host rewrites a frame file in T:/kosmos and the guest reads it per frame; 8 KB for a 128x128 tile, no serial limit [I]. - Effort M (needs a kosmos-app build, a render daemon, glue). Fidelity highest. Not replay-hashed. Licence MIT OR Apache-2.0.

R2. Host bake to sprite sheets the guest plays. Render turntables and clips offline (Blender is installed at /opt/homebrew/bin/blender, or kosmos-app --screenshot), quantise to a 16-colour palette per sprite, pack KSCHAR.BIN, blit with palette-swap tint. - Sizes [E]: 96x96 at 4 bpp is 4.6 KB raw, about 1.5 KB RLE. Hand, 8 yaw x 14 poses = 112 sprites, about 170 KB, plus 8 expression decals. Pip, 3 clips x 12 frames x 8 yaw = 288 frames, about 430 KB. Boot image: free; channel at 16 KB/s: 10 s once. - Cost per frame: one blit. Interaction: choose state, rotate by sprite, play a clip; no free camera, no new pose mixes. Kamiji's own protocol is discrete states (mood, status, emote), which map one to one onto sprites. - Byte-identical on x86 and Aiwnios, trivially hashable. Effort S for the hand, M for a library. Derived art stays CC BY-NC.

R3. Native skinned-mesh rasteriser in HolyC. Decimated rigs (hand about 1,500 tris, Pip about 3,000) in a compact binary (section 5), 16.16 fixed point, linear blend skinning with at most two bones per vertex, 16-bit z-buffer (painter's sort fails on overlapping fingers and hull outlines), flat or three-band toon shading, outline by drawing back faces pushed along the normal in ink colour (the trick in kamiji-toon.ts), no texture mapping (face as vertex colour plus decal sprites). - Realistic: hand at 160x160, 30 to 60 fps on x86; Pip at 192x192, 25 to 40 fps on x86, 60 on Aiwnios [E]. Texturing about doubles the pixel term; skip it. - Free orbit camera, any pose blend, finger curls from the same tables as Kamiji, picking with an id buffer. Integer-only code is bit-identical on both builds and aiwnios/golden.py, kernel-test.py can check it [R]. - Effort L: rasteriser about 400 lines (M), skin and clip player (M), decimation and converter (M). Risks: no continue, 10 ns calls, locals not zeroed on Aiwnios; use kslint.py.

R4. Engine sim/pose core as no_std in the guest. engine-on-templeos.md ran a Rust .BIN that matched host hashes at 25x slower [R], but for a hash and a float loop only; no engine crate was compiled for bare metal. Kamiji's pose logic is not in Rust, the engine hand rig is a different skeleton, and nothing here draws. Porting 14 curl tables to HolyC integers is cheaper. Aiwnios cannot load x86 .BINs. Effort L, value low.

R5. Aiwnios in-process C rasteriser. Register one native through PrsAddSymbol in aiwnios-sdl32.patch, as KsPresentRect and KsExec are [V]. A 43k-triangle hand at 60 fps is easy, but Aiwnios only. Effort M. Add after R3, as an optional power viewer.

R6. Kamiji's web component in host Chromium, shown as tiles. <kamiji-avatar> needs only an import map for three.js, serves from loopback, and its MCP e2e already runs in headless Chrome with SwiftShader [V]. docs/web-apps.md routes WebGL through tiles and plans an appd web run runner (not built) [V]. The avatar is 64 to 128 px, so a dirty rect is about 1 to 2 KB [E]. Gives the real Kamiji (all expressions, emotes, poses, shapes, protocol) with no guest rendering. Latency 100 to 200 ms; serial limits motion on x86. Effort S to M once the runner exists. Three.js is MIT.

4. The Kamiji UI kit#

What exists [V]: - kamiji/prototypes/ui-port is a prototype pipeline: JSON-schema contracts, --lf-* tokens (light and dark), five Lit components (lf-button, lf-badge, lf-card, lf-stat-card, lf-empty-state) plus 39 icons, a conformance runner, a Rust adapter folder. Its README: "Not a release". The roadmap says licence "provisional MIT", IP clearance open. - apps/os-styles/kamiji-ui/ is our vendored copy with three local token patches and the note "local prototyping only: do not publish". Our docs already say "do not ship until cleared". - The tokens are Longformer's. Our desk already has "Longformer light" and "Longformer dark" (ks_sn in 01-core.HC), and apps/os-styles/styles.css has nine styles. The values differ: styles.css accent #234184, ui-port accent #3b6fe0, desk3 slot 1 0x1F4FD8. "Kamiji UI" is a third blue, not the one we ship.

Mapping to desk3's 16 slots (ink accent ok teal bad purple warn surface muted accent2 card border shadow dock amber page): text-primary/secondary to ink/muted; accent/accent-hover to accent/accent2; success/danger/warning to ok/bad/warn; surface-1/2/canvas to card/surface/page; border-default to border; radii 6/8/12/16 to ks_rad and WinStyle geometry.

Missing: a toon "sticker" look (bold ink outline, #ffd23f, chubby corners) that the characters carry but the kit does not define [I: site CSS not audited]; hover, focus and dark states are not compared in the prototype; no table, tabs or dialog.

Effort: S to add colours as a sixth row (ks_pal, ks_sn, ks_kind, ks_rad, ks_glass, and every table sized by NS; grep first, ks_wallp[NS] and icon defaults are among them). Cheaper and in line with the other agents' work: author it as data in the Window Style editor (geometry, title bar, nine colour schemes; Export writes a ws1 ... line and B:/WINSTYLE.TXT) and the Icon Style editor, imported as a theme with no HolyC change [V window-style-editor.md, icon-style-editor.md]. Components: lf-badge, lf-stat-card, lf-empty-state are about 40 lines each in HolyC [E]; the widget kit has button, toggle, radio, slider. Icons are Lucide (ISC) and Feather (MIT), already in KSICONS.BIN. Do not publish while the kit is "provisional".

5. Asset pipeline#

5.1 Conversion#

Host tool scripts/kamiji_pack.py (Python 3, numpy and Pillow are present): 1. Read GLB JSON and BIN chunks: positions, normals, joints, weights, indices, inverse binds, node tree, animation samplers. 2. Decimate hand 41,744 to about 1,500 tris, Pip 10,818 to about 3,000, with Blender's Decimate modifier headless (blender -b -P) or quadric collapse; keep top two bone weights, renormalised to 8 bits. 3. Quantise positions to I16 in the bounding box; vertex colour to a palette index; no UVs. 4. Resample clips to 30 Hz; rotations as I16 quaternions; translation only for the root. 5. Face: eight expressions become 2D decals instead of 22 morph targets; hand shapeSphere/Cube become two baked vertex sets.

5.2 Format KMSH (little endian)#

Budgets [E]: hand (800 verts, 1,500 tris, 18 joints, 4 clips x 60 frames) about 55 KB; Pip (1,700 verts, 3,000 tris, 15 joints, 9 clips) about 70 KB; a 14-character roster about 1 MB. Heap is a non-issue (-m 1024M; the desktop's 2880x1800 buffers are about 20.7 MB each [R]). Pip's six face PNGs (3 to 4 KB) become 64x64 decals at 8 colours, about 500 bytes each.

5.3 Getting it in#

  • Boot image (precedent KSICONS.BIN, KSFONT2.BIN, built by gen-icons.py, gen-font2.py): no channel cost; best for the default hand and Pip (about 130 KB).
  • Channel for the long tail: scripts/send-to-vm.sh file push; 70 KB takes about 5 s at 16 KB/s, 1.5 s at 47 KB/s. Hub replies cap at 12,000 bytes, so use the file push, not HostCall.
  • Aiwnios: read from T:/kosmos/, no channel.
  • LFS: the engine tracks *.glb in LFS; assets/models is 252 MB, assets/human 612 MB, assets/kamikin 87 MB (mochi 14, planet 72). The kamiji repo's GLBs are plain files on disk. The packer reads from the kamiji repo and never touches LFS.

6. Phased plan with gates#

Day 1 prototype: R2 hand turntable#

Pick R2 over the Viewport wireframe, on evidence: (a) Viewport's F64 cubes already run, while wireframing a 41,744-triangle mesh needs decimation first, and that step pays off later in week 1 anyway; (b) a wireframe hand is not recognisably Kamiji, a tinted toon sprite is; (c) R2 has no frame-time risk and is byte-identical on both builds, which our golden tooling can check.

  1. scripts/kamiji_bake.py plus a Blender helper: blender -b -P ... on /Users/paulbloch/Documents/github/kamiji/assets/kamiji.glb; 24 yaw views of Idle and 8 frames of Wave; 96x96, transparent, Workbench with flat light [I: untried; EEVEE may need a GPU].
  2. Quantise to 16 colours (Pillow), RLE-pack into assets/KSCHAR.BIN using stream.py enc 0.
  3. desk3/03m-characters.HC: loader, SpriteBlit(x,y,id,tint), drag-to-rotate, Idle/Wave buttons; hook as app 16.
  4. Test with scripts/snap.sh and screenshots to shots/characters-*.png; Aiwnios via aiwnios/run-desk.sh; hash both.

Gate: smooth rotation by mouse on both builds, tint swap works, identical sprite decode, under 3 ms a frame. Approvals needed: running Blender (installed, no download), a new 120 KB binary, the NA raise and AppBody hook in shared files. No LFS, no cargo.

Week 1 MVP: R3 on hand and Pip#

scripts/kamiji_pack.py and KMSH writer; desk3/03n-raster.HC (fixed-point rasteriser, z-buffer, skin, clip player); the app prefers the mesh and falls back to sprites; protocol v1 through the hub; screenshot button. Gate: hand at 30 fps or better and Pip at 25 fps or better on x86 QEMU in a 192x192 viewport; identical frame hash on x86 and Aiwnios for a scripted clip; kslint.py clean; silhouettes match the bake within tolerance.

Three months#

All 14 mochi and the rubbernose cast from a character folder (manifest shape of applications-folder.md), expression decals, emotes, the 14 poses as a data file ported from the TypeScript tables, hover reactions, the status-bar mood driven by agents over MCP, optional R6 or R1 live viewer on the web-app runner, a Kamiji theme imported through the style editors. Gate: owner sign-off on licences and provenance before anything leaves the machine.

Risks#

Licence and provenance (Meshy, mochi) block any release; shared-file collisions with other agents; speeds are modelled, the day-one blit is the first real number; decimating a 26k-vertex chubby hand may lose fingers; headless Blender on macOS may need a GPU, with kosmos-app --screenshot (a cargo build, so approval) as fallback; avoid F64 in the rasteriser.

7. Verified versus inferred#

Verified: repo list, commit counts, stacks; GLB statistics; poses, expressions, emotes living in TypeScript; protocol v1 and MCP bridge as documented; licence texts and caveats; the vendored kit and its patches; desk3 style tables (NS 5, NA 16); Viewport and Scene source; stream.py encodings; the PrsAddSymbol pattern; Blender, numpy, Pillow present; kosmos-app flags; HolyC micro-benchmarks as cited.

Inferred or estimated: that Projects' "Kamiji" means the characters (85 percent); every rasteriser cost and frame rate; RLE sizes; Aiwnios 4x to 10x speed-up (the brief's 40 to 60x is in no repo doc); the Aiwnios frame-file route; headless Blender rendering; that the UI kit lacks a toon look (site CSS not read); 47 KB/s serial (reported) against the brief's 16 KB/s.

Not examined: kamiji/site CSS, integrations/openclaw-os, longformer, core-design-library, the Kamikin game, prototypes/ui-port/rust.
